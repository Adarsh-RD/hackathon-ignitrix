Section 44AD — Presumptive Taxation for Small Businesses
This section allows eligible businesses with turnover up to Rs 2 crore to declare income at a prescribed rate without maintaining detailed books of accounts. For digital receipts (UPI, bank transfer), only 6% of gross receipts is treated as taxable profit. For cash receipts, 8% is treated as taxable profit. This is highly beneficial for gig workers receiving payments digitally via Swiggy, Zomato, Ola, Rapido, Uber.

Section 194-O — TDS on E-Commerce Transactions
E-commerce operators like Swiggy, Zomato, Ola, Uber, Rapido are required to deduct TDS at the rate of 1% on the gross amount of sales or services facilitated through their platform. This applies when the total amount exceeds Rs 5 lakh in a financial year. The gig worker can claim credit for this TDS while filing their ITR.

Section 37(1) — Business Expenditure Deduction
Any expenditure that is not capital in nature and is incurred wholly and exclusively for the purpose of business or profession is allowed as a deduction. For gig workers, this includes: petrol and fuel costs, mobile phone recharge, internet bills, vehicle maintenance and service charges, insurance for vehicle used for work, toll charges, and any other expense directly related to delivery or ride-share work.

Section 80C — Deductions under Chapter VI-A
Under the old tax regime, individuals can claim deductions up to Rs 1.5 lakh for investments in ELSS mutual funds, PPF, NSC, tax-saving fixed deposits, life insurance premiums, tuition fees for children, and home loan principal repayment. This is NOT available under the new tax regime.

New Tax Regime Slabs FY 2025-26
The new tax regime offers lower rates without deductions. Income up to Rs 3 lakh is tax-free. Rs 3 lakh to Rs 7 lakh is taxed at 5%. Rs 7 lakh to Rs 10 lakh at 10%. Rs 10 lakh to Rs 12 lakh at 15%. Rs 12 lakh to Rs 15 lakh at 20%. Above Rs 15 lakh at 30%. A standard deduction of Rs 75,000 is available. Rebate under Section 87A makes income up to Rs 7 lakh effectively tax-free.

Old Tax Regime Slabs FY 2025-26
Income up to Rs 2.5 lakh is tax-free. Rs 2.5 lakh to Rs 5 lakh at 5%. Rs 5 lakh to Rs 10 lakh at 20%. Above Rs 10 lakh at 30%. But deductions under 80C, 80D, HRA, LTA etc. are available. Rebate under 87A for income up to Rs 5 lakh.

ITR-4 (Sugam) — Tax Return Form for Presumptive Income
Gig workers opting for Section 44AD should file ITR-4. This is a simplified form for individuals and HUFs having income from business under presumptive taxation. The due date is usually July 31 of the assessment year. If turnover exceeds Rs 1 crore (Rs 2 crore for digital), a tax audit under Section 44AB is required.

Health and Education Cess
A cess of 4% is levied on the total income tax amount. This applies to both old and new tax regime. It is calculated after computing tax based on applicable slab rates.

Advance Tax for Gig Workers
If total tax liability in a financial year exceeds Rs 10,000, advance tax must be paid in quarterly installments. Due dates: 15 June (15%), 15 September (45%), 15 December (75%), 15 March (100%). Failure to pay advance tax attracts interest under Section 234B and 234C.

TDS Credit and Refund
If TDS deducted by platforms exceeds the actual tax liability (common for small earners), the gig worker is entitled to a refund. The refund can be claimed by filing ITR and will be processed by the Income Tax Department, usually within 3-6 months.

GST for Gig Workers
Gig workers with aggregate turnover exceeding Rs 20 lakh (Rs 10 lakh for special category states) are required to register for GST. Most delivery partners fall below this threshold. If registered, they can opt for composition scheme (1% tax on turnover) for simplicity.

Section 44ADA — Presumptive Taxation for Professionals
For professionals (freelancers, consultants) with gross receipts up to Rs 50 lakh, 50% of gross receipts is deemed as profit. This is different from 44AD which uses 6%/8%. Gig workers doing delivery are classified as business, not profession.

Platform-Specific Tax Treatment
Swiggy and Zomato delivery partners receive income classified as business income. Ola and Uber driver partners also earn business income. Rapido and other bike-taxi partners fall under the same category. All of them can use Section 44AD with 6% deemed profit rate for digital payments.
